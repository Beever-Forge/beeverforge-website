The planned Enterprise workflow

A decision chain, with context.

A client brings the idea. Each stage adds context and passes a concrete output to the next. Portfolio Manager returns after architecture to consolidate the handoff before the Product Owner takes over.

  1. 01

    Prototype Builder

    Enterprise

    Describe the idea. Explore the prototype.

    The client shapes a prototype through a conversation, refining the experience before engineering work begins.

    Output: prototype and captured requirements
  2. 02

    Requirements handoff

    Enterprise

    Carry the intent forward.

    Prototype Builder hands a structured requirements list to Portfolio Manager so the next stage can work from the original intent.

    Output: a requirements package
  3. 03

    Portfolio Manager

    Enterprise

    Read the company, not just the request.

    Retrieves context across projects, talks with the client, identifies relationships and refines requirements against the company’s existing landscape.

    Output: requirements in company context
  4. 04

    Virtual Architect

    Enterprise

    Make the technical decisions explicit.

    Analyzes the proposed work, designs the solution and records architectural decisions in ADRs.

    Output: solution design and decision records
  5. 05

    Portfolio Manager

    Enterprise

    Close the loop before execution.

    Reconciles the architecture and requirements, closes the remaining alignment and prepares the handoff to the Product Owner.

    Output: a consolidated delivery handoff
  6. 06

    Product Owner

    Shared core

    Turn aligned intent into delivery work.

    Receives the handoff and continues through the existing product workflow: requirements, acceptance and work prepared for engineering.

    Output: work ready for the engineering core
  7. 07

    Engineering core

    Shared core

    Implement. Verify. Review.

    Orchestrates coding agents, runs the project’s checks and obtains an independent review. Merge follows your policy; production requires human approval.

    Output: tested, reviewed changes

Enterprise stages are in development. The Product Owner and engineering workflow are part of the existing core.

A few useful distinctions

Before you choose.

Will Community remain open source?

Yes. Community will remain available as the open-source foundation of BeeverForge. Its Apache-2.0 engineering core is available today; Enterprise is a paid edition built around that foundation.

What does Enterprise add?

The planned Enterprise edition adds conversational prototyping, a structured requirements handoff, Portfolio Manager, Virtual Architect, SSO, RBAC and BeeverForge support. It connects the work before engineering with the core delivery workflow.

Is Enterprise available today?

Enterprise is in development. The workflow shown here describes the planned product experience. Contact us to discuss your use case, requirements and commercial interest. Pricing and launch availability have not yet been announced.

Does Community include a Product Owner?

Yes. The existing core includes a Product Owner requirements workflow. Enterprise adds the upstream prototype, portfolio and architecture process that hands work to that Product Owner.

What does free Community mean?

The Community core is licensed under Apache-2.0. You operate it on your infrastructure and use your own coding agents and credentials. Infrastructure and model-provider costs are separate.
